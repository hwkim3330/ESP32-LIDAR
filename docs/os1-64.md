# The 64 beam sensor does not fit through a W5500

The OS1-16 worked here for a day. Its replacement, an **OS-1-64**, does not, and the reasons are
structural rather than a matter of tuning.

## Why it fragments, and why that is fatal

A column is 12 bytes of header plus four per pixel. Sixteen beams make a 76 byte column; sixty-four
make 268. The sensor will not send fewer than sixteen columns in a packet —

```
columns_per_packet must be a positive multiple of 16
```

— so the smallest datagram an OS1-64 emits is `32 + 16*268 + 32 = 4352` bytes, which is three IP
fragments on any 1500 byte link. There is no setting that avoids it.

And this board cannot receive a fragmented datagram at all:

```
# CONFIG_LWIP_IP4_REASSEMBLY is not set        (Arduino ESP32 libraries)
#define IP_REASSEMBLY 0                        (lwipopts.h)
```

**Reassembly is compiled out.** Fragments are discarded on arrival, always, at any rate. The
symptom is a saturated wire and a socket that reads zero, and narrowing the azimuth window changes
how many arrive rather than whether they survive — measured at 45°, 90°, 180° and 360°, all zero.

**This also corrects an earlier conclusion.** Yesterday the OS1-16 at `1024x10` with the full
profile — 3392 byte datagrams, also fragmented — produced the same saturated-wire-zero-packets
symptom, and it was written up as the board being overwhelmed by fragment rate. It was not. Those
datagrams were being discarded on arrival for exactly this reason, and no amount of slowing them
down would have helped.

## Reassembling them here works, but costs the rest of the stack

`reassemble.h` takes the driver's input path, puts the sensor's fragments back together, and hands
completed datagrams to the same decoder a socket would have fed. It works: **25,039 fragments
became 8,345 datagrams**, three to one as expected, two abandoned, with a flat heap.

Then everything else stops. With the input path taken over, lwIP receives nothing — the switch
stops answering CoAP, the sensor stops answering HTTP, and about thirty seconds later the sensor's
ARP entry for this board expires and even the stream being reassembled dies. The passthrough for
frames this code does not consume is wrong somewhere, and until it is right the feature costs more
than it buys. It is behind `R` on the console rather than on by default.

## The second core does its half perfectly; the chip does not

Decoding was moved off the driver's thread — the callback now copies a datagram into one of two
slots and swaps, and a task pinned to the other core unpacks it. That half works completely:
**zero abandoned, zero dropped**, the decoder never once behind at 320 datagrams a second.

It changes nothing about the failure. With reassembly running the board takes the stream cleanly
for **twelve to fourteen seconds** — 3,900 datagrams from 11,700 fragments, heap flat — and then
stops dead, every time, at every azimuth window tried:

| window | datagrams | how long it lasted | SPI errors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 180° | 4,632 at 93/s | 14 seconds of 47 | 23 |
| 360° | 3,900 at 320/s | 12 seconds | 31 |

What stops it is the chip:

```
w5500_spi_read(166): spi transmit failed
emac_w5500_receive(750): read payload failed, len=1434, offset=48102
```

**48102 is past the end of a 16 kB receive buffer.** The buffer overflows because the driver
cannot drain 1500 byte frames over SPI as fast as they arrive, the read pointer desynchronises,
and from that moment the driver is wedged permanently — it retries the same impossible read
forever, and lwIP receives nothing either. Narrowing the window to 180° halves the rate and buys
two more seconds.

So transmit and receive are not symmetric on this part. Eight megabits a second of 1500 byte
frames go **out** without a single FCS error over three minutes; a third of that coming **in**
overflows it in twelve seconds. Sending is the chip's own buffer emptying at line rate; receiving
is the host having to keep up, and the host is a serial bus.

## And the SPI does not survive this traffic either

Under the OS1-64's stream the driver reports its own trouble:

```
w5500_spi_read(166): spi transmit failed
emac_w5500_receive(750): read payload failed, len=1434, offset=48102
```

Continuously at 40 MHz; **17 errors in 81 seconds at 20 MHz**. Yesterday's stability measurement —
three minutes at 40 MHz, 117,562 frames, not one corrupt — was taken with 8 Mbit/s of *transmit*
and a small, unfragmented receive. This is a different load, and the clock that was right for the
old sensor is not right for this one. The SPI clock is not a property of the board alone.

## What this means for the vehicle

For the SCOUT MINI build, **the LiDAR should go straight to the Jetson**, which has real gigabit
and a stack that reassembles. A W5500 on an ESP32-S3 tops out around 8 Mbit/s of unfragmented
traffic; an OS1-64 at its slowest setting is 11 Mbit/s of fragmented traffic. It is the wrong end
of the wire for this sensor.

That does not remove the board from the design — it moves it. The ESP and the LAN9662 belong in
the path as the TSN element: shaping, gating and measuring the traffic that shares the network with
the sensor, which is what the last two days measured them doing well. What they are not is the
sensor's receiver.
